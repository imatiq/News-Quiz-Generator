Title: Medicine prices soar up to 99% after deregulation
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2641941/medicine-prices-soar-up-to-99-after-deregulation
Content:
Pharmaceutical companies have made a massive increase of up to 99% in prices of medicines following market deregulation, said the Competition Commission of Pakistan (CCP) in its latest study and recommended targeted probes instead of reversing deregulation.
The CCP revealed that pharma firms had raised prices of eye treatment medicines up to 99.3%. Prices for skincare medicines had been increased by 77.9% while those of minerals and multivitamins went up 64.8%. The government had deregulated prices of non-essential medicines in February 2024, while allowing drug manufacturers to determine prices in line with prevailing market dynamics. Prices of essential medicines, covered in the National Essential Medicines List, however, are regulated under the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan's (DRAP) Drug Pricing Policy 2018.
The commission recommended continued monitoring and targeted investigations instead of reversing the deregulation policy. It called for establishing a public registry of medicine price changes, requiring companies to inform the regulator whenever they revised prices and directing DRAP to publish the changes in a centralised database. The system will enable consumers to track medicine prices over time.
It stressed that DRAP should evaluate price hikes against actual inflationary trends and ask manufacturers to explain the increase that exceeded specified thresholds. "This will help identify unusual price adjustments."
The commission proposed targeted investigations into exceptional price increases, particularly in those medicines with limited alternatives or a small number of suppliers. "Such reviews should examine manufacturing and import costs, exchange rate movements and the number of competing brands to establish whether higher prices reflect increased costs or limited market competition," it said.
According to the report, the fertility medicine prices rose 7.5%, diabetes and endocrinology medicine prices increased 23.6% and neurology and psychiatry medicine prices spiked 24.7%. Among products that recorded price hikes of more than 50%, GlaxoSmithKline accounted for 32 products, Martin Dow Marker 22, Abbott 17, Searle and Novartis/ Nouveau 16 each, and Sami Pharmaceuticals 15.
The study, titled "Deregulation and its Impact on Pharmaceutical Prices in Pakistan: An Empirical Investigation from 2023-26", found that 85.6% of the 822 non-essential medicines examined had shown price increases. Around 14% remained unchanged and only 0.2% registered a decline.
The report found that 31.3%, or 257, products exhibited price increases of more than 50%, while 7.7%, or 63, products recorded spikes exceeding 100%. The cumulative median increase came in at 33.9% between 2023 and 2026, which the commission said was below the overall inflation rate during the same period. Following recommendation from the Senate Secretariat on May 13, 2026, the CCP had initiated the study on June 3 to examine post-deregulation medicine prices and determine whether the pricing behaviour reflected competitive market conditions or possible cartelisation.
The commission employed quantitative and qualitative analysis. It examined yearly price data from the first quarter of 2023 to the first quarter of 2026. The research covered 500 of the most-selling pharmaceutical brands identified through DRAP's National Pharmaceutical Pricing Survey 2025-26 and examined 822 non-essential products using the data obtained from Pharmaguide Systems.
According to the report, the median medicine prices jumped up 14.3% in 2023-24, 11.4% in 2024-25 and 5.2% in 2025-26. In comparison, the consumer price inflation stood at 29.2% in 2023, 23.4% in 2024, 4.5% in 2025 and 6.2% during the first quarter of 2026. The commission concluded that the median increase in medicine prices did not exceed the overall inflation during the period under review. It said that average price increases were not unusual.
Pakistan's pharmaceutical sector caters to a population of around 250 million and comprises approximately 690 active local and multinational companies. Around 85% of finished medicines are manufactured locally, although nearly 95% of raw materials are imported.

Title: Provinces denied Rs1.8tr profits
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2641938/provinces-denied-rs18tr-profits
Content:
The federal government did not transfer Rs1.8 trillion in profits of four oil and gas sector companies to the provinces in breach of the Constitution, and also promoted two out of every three Grade-22 officers from Punjab, revealed proceedings of a Senate panel working to enforce the Constitution.
Out of the 53 bureaucrats holding the highest Grade-22, as many as 35 belonged to Punjab, 10 to Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa (K-P), only six to Sindh, and one each from Balochistan and Gilgit-Baltistan (G-B), revealed details shared by the Establishment Division on Monday.
The two glaring findings that the federal government shared with the subcommittee of the Senate Functional Committee on Devolution pointed towards deprivations that caused resentment among the smaller federating units against the Centre. PPP Senator Zamir Hussain Ghumro chaired the meeting to review the implementation of constitutional Article 172(3), which deals with equal ownership of the oil and gas sector, and Article 38(g), which ensures the due share of the provinces in federal government jobs.
Since 2011, only four companies - Pakistan Petroleum Limited (PPL), Oil and Gas Development Company (OGDCL), Mari Petroleum and Pakistan Oil Fields Limited (POL) - earned Rs3.6 trillion in profits after the 18th amendment, and not a single penny has been paid to the provinces against their Rs1.8 trillion share, said Ghumro. According to these details, the federal government did not transfer half of the shares, which came to Rs978 billion in the case of OGDCL, Rs462 billion of PPL, Rs225 billion of Mari and Rs144 billion of POL.
Article 172(3) ensures joint and equal ownership and sharing of resources, which has not been implemented in letter and spirit, said the chairman, while emphasising that the provinces are constitutionally entitled to an equal share in revenues generated from oil and gas resources produced within their territories. Expressing serious concern over the non-implementation of Article 172(3), Senator Ghumro termed the situation a violation of the Constitution and stated that the continued deprivation of provinces of their legitimate financial share is unacceptable.
The continued revelations made in various meetings of the committee highlight the reasons fuelling resentment among provinces like Balochistan and Sindh and also show the dominance of Punjab-origin bureaucrats in the federal government. The federal government has also spent Rs8.5 trillion on provincial matters since 2011, including Rs3.5 trillion on the Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP), despite these matters relating to provinces.
Ghumro directed the Petroleum Division to share a framework for transferring half of the total profits of the oil and gas sector companies with the provinces. The total amount may exceed Rs6 trillion when the details of other companies are received, said the chairman of the committee. He directed the Petroleum Division to place the framework before the federal cabinet for approval on an urgent basis and share the relevant summary with the subcommittee. He warned that failure to comply with constitutional requirements could lead to referral of the matter to the auditing authorities of Pakistan for further examination.
"All the chief ministers of the provinces and the federal ministers for finance and petroleum shall be called in the meeting if the matter remains unresolved," according to the decision by the subcommittee.
The committee asked the Auditor General to comply with Article 172(3) while auditing the profits of the oil and gas corporations.
Provincial share in jobs
The Establishment Division shared details of provincial shares in federal government jobs, which showed that Punjab and K-P had higher than constitutional shares at the expense of Sindh and Balochistan.
Article 38(g) of the Constitution states: "Ensure that the shares of the provinces in all federal services, including autonomous bodies and corporations established by, or under the control of, the federal government shall be secured and any omission in the allocation of the shares of the provinces in the past shall be rectified."
According to the formula, in the case of direct recruitment, 7.5% of positions will be filled on merit. As much as 50% will be the share of Punjab, including Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT), 19% for Sindh, 11.5% for K-P, 6% for Balochistan, 1% for G-B, 3% for erstwhile Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) and 2% for Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK).
However, according to the Establishment Division, there were 53 positions of Grade-22 in the federal secretariat and 35, or 66%, were occupied by bureaucrats from Punjab. Sindh got only six positions, K-P 10, Balochistan one and G-B one, but there were no bureaucrats in Grade-22 from AJK and erstwhile FATA. There seems to be an error in the data, as Inter-Provincial Coordination Secretary Mohyuddin Wani belongs to AJK.
The functional committee's interpretation is that appointments are direct induction, through transfers and promotion to the next grade, saying that provincial shares should be observed in all three cases. The Establishment Division informed the committee that in March this year, it had issued instructions to all ministries, divisions and concerned organisations to ensure "strict observance of the prescribed provincial shares".
"Based on the detailed grade-wise analysis conducted by the Establishment Division, the representation of all provinces and regions in the federal government services is generally consistent with the prescribed allocated shares and remains broadly balanced across various grades, except in the case of Balochistan," according to the Establishment Division.
The chairman of the subcommittee said the rural-urban divide of Sindh was against the provision of Article 38(g), which envisages shares of provinces, and directed that the urban-rural divide be done away with, maintaining only the share of the whole province like other provinces.
As against its 50% share, overall Punjab has 60.17% of positions in the federal secretariat and K-P 16%, well above its share of 11.5%.
But Sindh got only 14.5% of total jobs as against its share of 19% and Balochistan 4% compared to its 6% share. The Establishment Division informed the subcommittee that 5,136 posts in BPS 1-15 had recently been advertised for Balochistan to address its employment needs.
The division also apprised the subcommittee of various exemptions granted by the federal cabinet to facilitate applicants from Balochistan and its plan to create 200,000 employment opportunities for the province. The convener emphasised that recruitment arrangements must ensure fair opportunities for candidates from deserving households and regions.
The convener expressed displeasure over the Establishment Division's failure to submit the rectification plan for Sindh, as directed in earlier meetings.

Title: USA joint bid and UK confirmed as 2031 and 2035 Women's World Cup hosts
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2642008/usa-joint-bid-and-uk-confirmed-as-2031-and-2035-womens-world-cup-hosts
Content:
FIFA on Monday confirmed that a joint bid led by the United States will host the expanded, 48-team Women's World Cup in 2031, while the United Kingdom will host the following edition in 2035.
The winning bids were waved through by acclamation after FIFA president Gianni Infantino confirmed the USA in partnership with Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica was the "sole candidacy" to host in 2031, when the tournament will feature 48 teams for the first time.
The joint English, Scottish, Northern Irish and Welsh bid was also the only candidacy for 2035.
Infantino had already revealed last year that the respective bids for 2031 and 2035 were unchallenged and therefore set to be awarded the tournaments, with both being officially rubber-stamped at a virtual gathering of FIFA's member associations.
"As of 2031 there will be 48 teams and four hosts," said Infantino. "This is an incredible growth in just a few years."
The next Women's World Cup will take place next year in Brazil, running from June 24 to July 25. It will feature 32 teams, with the final at the Maracana Stadium in Rio de Janeiro.
Spain go into that tournament as the holders after winning the last Women's World Cup held in Australia and New Zealand in 2023, when they defeated current European champions England in the final.
The US co-hosted this year's men's World Cup, with the majority of matches being played on American soil, including the final at the MetLife Stadium in New Jersey which was won by Spain. That was the first men's edition featuring 48 teams.
The USA has hosted the Women's World Cup twice before, in 1999 and 2003, and is also the most successful nation in the tournament's history, having lifted the trophy four times.
'Landmark moment'
US Soccer reacted to the announcement by saying the awarding of the 2031 tournament gave the country the chance to "build on the momentum" of this year's World Cup as well as the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. The United States are the reigning Olympic women's football champions.
No senior men's or women's World Cup has been played in the UK since England hosted and won the men's edition in 1966.A joint statement by the four UK national associations described the announcement as "a landmark moment".
It is not yet known which cities will host matches at the respective tournaments, but most matches in 2031 are likely to take place in the United States while England can expect to have the lion's share of venues in 2035.
The UK bid proposed 22 stadiums in total, with 16 in England, three in Wales, two in Scotland and one in Northern Ireland.
Scottish Football Association chief executive Ian Maxwell said the two venues in the country would be the 52,000-seat Hampden Park in Glasgow as well as Hibernian's 20,000-seat Easter Road home in Edinburgh.
The draw for the 2027 Women's World Cup will take place on Friday, December 11, in Rio de Janeiro. The USA and England are among the nations aiming to secure qualification for the finals before then.
Infantino said Monday's Congress was organised "to celebrate women's football" and was "dedicated exclusively to that".
He therefore did not address his bid for re-election as the head of world football's governing body.
The election will take place at the next FIFA Congress to be held in the Moroccan capital Rabat in March, with France's Philippe Diallo and Japan's Kozo Tashima standing against the incumbent.

Title: Climate shifts slash olive yields in Khyber
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2641985/climate-shifts-slash-olive-yields-in-khyber
Content:
Climate change and erratic weather are cutting into olive production across Khyber district, leaving growers with smaller harvests and less oil even as official efforts expand the crop.
Farmers report that excessive rains in March and April, the critical flowering period, disrupted fruit set this year. The result has been lower olive yields and a noticeable drop in oil output. Ahmad Gul, who grows about 1,100 olive trees on 20 acres in Shah Kas, Tehsil Jamrud, told The Express Tribune that production has fallen sharply compared with last year. "Earlier we used to get more than 120 litres of oil from the orchard. Now it is less than 100 litres," he said. He attributes the decline primarily to climate change and unusual rainfall patterns. Gul also noted that olive trees face various diseases and called on the agriculture department to supply timely sprays, pesticides and urea so orchards can be protected.
Similar concerns come from Bara tehsil. Ziarat Gul Afridi, who manages 140 trees in Spah Dora, said the department planted olive orchards in the area about seven years ago. Early yields were encouraging, but climate change and irregular weather have since reduced output. He stressed that sustained official attention and practical assistance are needed to keep production viable. Fazal Rabi Afridi, owner of an 800-tree orchard in Akakhel, likewise reported a marked drop this season linked to changing weather. He has sprayed against diseases at his own expense and said that while the department provides occasional training on orchard care, more concrete support, medicines, sprays and other inputs, is essential.
Grafting of wild olive plants continues in areas such as Takhtaki in Tirah and Payanda China in Upper Bara, though regular commercial production has yet to begin in those locations.
According to Khyber district Director of Agriculture Ziaul Islam Dawar, 415 acres of olive orchards have been established under the merged-districts olive promotion project. Roughly half of these are in Bara tehsil (Akakhel, Spah, Qambar Zaw and Shin Drang among other sites). Jamrud (Ghandi, Shah Kas, Jalaluddin and Malagori) and Landi Kotal (Shalman and adjoining areas) are also covered. This year's harvest from these orchards yielded about 3,500 litres of oil. Large-scale grafting has converted wild olive stock into improved varieties; project records show 178,938 of a targeted 195,000 wild plants have already been grafted, with roughly 16,000 still to be done. The original target for new orchards was 1,402 acres.
A modern processing unit has been installed at the Agriculture Training Centre in Bara. It can process 200 kg of olives per hour and is equipped with a 35 kVA generator, filters and related machinery, giving local growers a nearby facility to extract oil and strengthen the crop's commercial value.
Ten training programmes were planned to build farmers' skills in grafting, orchard management, disease control, value addition, market access and nursery development. Three have been completed; seven remain. Farmers registered with Farmers Service Centres receive priority for new orchard schemes, and both individual growers (one acre or more) and groups (minimum five to eight acres) can apply through local agriculture officers.
Wild olive resources remain substantial. Across the former FATA districts an estimated 53.45 million wild plants exist; Khyber alone accounts for about 5.5 million, found in Tor Wela, Mastak, Bazaar Zakha Khel, Tarkho Kas, Nara Baba, Payanda China, Mehrban Kalle, Sandana, Surkas and other locations. Converting these through grafting offers a long-term route to higher production.
Agricultural experts note that successful olive yields depend on suitable weather during flowering and fruit set, proper irrigation, timely disease diagnosis and scientific orchard management. Officials are urged to factor climate risks into guidance, maintain regular monitoring and ensure farmers receive sprays, fertilisers and other inputs without delay.
The olive project holds clear potential to strengthen the local economy, create new income streams for farmers and increase domestic edible-oil production. Yet planting new trees alone will not suffice. Protecting existing orchards, raising yields, producing quality oil and linking growers to markets remain equally critical if the initiative is to deliver lasting results.

Title: A final flight of courage: Pakistan's 1st female fighter pilot remembered
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2642002/a-final-flight-of-courage-pakistans-1st-female-fighter-pilot-remembered
Content:
Some acts of courage outlive the moment they are performed. Eleven years after Marium Mukhtiar lost her life while attempting to save civilians from a malfunctioning fighter jet, Pakistan continues to remember the country's first woman fighter pilot for a sacrifice that became part of its national story.
Mukhtiar embraced martyrdom on November 24, 2015, during a training flight with her instructor when a technical fault developed in their aircraft. With the prospect of ejecting before them, she tried to steer the stricken jet away from a populated area, putting civilian lives ahead of her own.
Her courage and sacrifice were recognised by the government, which posthumously awarded her Tamgha-e-Basalat in 2016.
Born in Karachi on May 18, 1992, Mukhtiar joined the Pakistan Air Force's 132nd GD Pilot Course in May 2011, after completing her early education. She had chosen to follow in her father's footsteps by joining the armed forces, taking on the demanding challenge of becoming a fighter pilot.
After completing the initial phase of her training at PAF Academy Risalpur, she was sent to MM Alam Air Base for fighter conversion training. She was on the path to joining the ranks of fighter pilots entrusted with defending the country's skies.
On the morning of November 24, 2015, Mukhtiar took off with her instructor for a routine training mission. The flight turned tragic when the aircraft developed a technical fault. As the pilots faced the need to eject, the risk to people on the ground became a critical concern.
In an attempt to take the aircraft away from the residential area, Mukhtiar lost her life.
More than a decade later, her story remains a reminder of the courage and sense of duty that define service in the armed forces. Mukhtiar's legacy also continues to inspire women aspiring to break barriers in professions traditionally dominated by men.

Title: Smartphone imports put 40,000 jobs at risk
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2641944/smartphone-imports-put-40000-jobs-at-risk
Content:
Imports of used smartphones surged to 98,383 units in October 2026 from just 1,434 in July, as narrowing duty differences made imports more attractive.
During July 2026, 18,431 smartphones valued at $17.8 million were imported in completely built-up (CBU) condition, whereas in October, 150,133 units valued at $71.7 million were imported.
The Pakistan Mobile Phone Manufacturers Association (PMPMA) has expressed concerns to the Ministry of Industries and Production as well as the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) over the development, highlighting that the most alarming issue was the jump in imports of used smartphones from 1,434 units in July to 98,383 units in October 2026.
This is clearly having a drastically negative impact on smartphone manufacturers, who have seen their production drop from 906,711 units in July 2026 to 752,870 units in October 2026, a reduction of around 17%.
This has caused serious concern among the domestic industry, which employs a direct and indirect workforce of over 40,000 and produces almost all global brands in Pakistan. Nearly all smartphone manufacturers have reported layoffs and retrenchment of skilled workers to reduce costs.
Industry sources point to two reasons for this surge in CBU imports.
The first is that the tariff gap between CBU smartphones and completely knocked-down (CKD) kits for locally assembled smartphones has narrowed sharply to around 11% on a $100 phone and 14% on a $200 phone.
The second reason is a downward revision in FBR valuations issued in January 2026, which has made it much cheaper to import used smartphones at under-invoiced values, causing a loss to the national exchequer.
For the last three years, industry stakeholders have been complaining about reverse cascading, which has made it impossible to localise parts such as chargers, batteries, cables and motherboard assembly.
Currently, a charger is importable at 0% duty as part of a CKD kit, but the cumulative duty impact of importing its raw material and subcomponents is nearly 20%.
This makes localisation unfeasible, and the industry has repeatedly requested the government to rectify this policy discrepancy as it would help reduce logistic and financial costs, hence making exports viable.
Industry sources also cited the example of the Indian smartphone industry, which, from the level of near-zero exports in 2020, is touching almost $30 billion in exports in fiscal year 2025-26.
This has been achieved with the government's support towards local value addition through favourable policies and Production Linked Incentives.
A similar strategy should also be followed by Pakistan to take advantage of the transformative potential of mobile manufacturing, as seen in China, Vietnam and India.
It is an undeniable fact that CBU-imported smartphones have now captured 41.8% of the smartphone market size by value, hurting job creation and investment in this strategic industry.

Title: No longer curious
Date: 2026-11-24
Source: https://tribune.com.pk/
URL: https://tribune.com.pk/story/2641920/no-longer-curious
Content:
Last week, two completely unrelated events made me reflect on perhaps the most serious challenge (at least in my opinion) in research today. It is not about a particular problem, or a grand challenge, or an ethical conundrum. It is about the nature of research itself. I am increasingly concerned that researchers at all levels – students, early career scholars and senior researchers – no longer value curiosity. I am worried that research is increasingly become transactional (projects lead to papers, papers lead to promotion or grants), or that research process has become algorithmic (doing these four series of experiments equals a paper, four separate papers make a PhD). As a result, research at least in the areas that I am familiar with is becoming increasingly predictable, driven by goals that have little to do with inquiry and more to do with it being another task with a list of sub-tasks in order to get a product (a degree, promotion, etc).
The first of the two independent events was a meeting with a professor of modern literature who was visiting from another institution. She was deeply worried that universities are no longer interested in trying new things or taking any chances. This colleague was concerned that universities are investing only in programmes that have a tangible (i.e. short-term financial) return on investment, or chasing a popularity fad, and investments in programmes that create serious thinking, or give space to students and faculty to imagine a new set of questions is eroding fast. Creativity is measured by the speed of output, and while there are lots and lots of papers, few ask new questions. And because for a variety of reasons (incentives, pressures, attitudes) professors are not interested in being curious, students have little reason to change status quo.
The second event was the passing of one of the most prominent chemists of the last half century, George Whitesides. Professor Whitesides spent most of his career at Harvard and was a giant in the field. I never met him in person, but had communicated with him during my graduate school as I benefitted from his papers and always had questions for him. He always responded to my emails (sometimes within a few minutes!) and was always kind, thoughtful and encouraging in his response. On his passing, there have been many tributes from colleagues and friends from around the world who were fortunate to have him as a mentor. In one of those tributes, I came across one of his essays (published in 2018) titled "Curiosity and Science". In the essay, he argues why curiosity is fundamental to science, and what makes it so special by virtue of it being both effortless and most serious at the same time. I had not seen the essay before, and I will make sure that it is a required reading in all of my future classes.
At the end of the essay he says "as with many activities that are competing for time and attention in a utilitarian world curiosity can atrophy from neglect. It can certainly be unfocused and lead to nothing (or at least nothing immediately useful) but using it as the starting point for careful observation of nature and society is a nontrivial skill and a starting point for new intellectual endeavors and adventures. It is one essential contributor to creativity in science and a start in forcing new ideas into inflexible professional orthodoxies. What happens to science without it? My students occasionally (for other reasons) ask 'What is the one novel I must read?' I answer '1984.' That is a world without curiosity. Or science."
Today, I feel that academic institutions both in the country where I live and the country where I am from, there is a fundamental disappearance of curiosity. In the classrooms, labs, libraries or archives, our path is taking us to predictable answers, in part because our search is not aimed at finding answers, but a marketable product. That, to me, is a betrayal of what is the most beautiful thing about the life of the mind that the university is supposed to cultivate.

Meta's chief AI officer Alexandr Wang has said that artificial intelligence capable of surpassing human intellect is on the way, warning that decisions over the next year will be critical to how humanity handles it.
“Superintelligence is arriving. How humanity acts over the next year will have great consequence on what future world we live in,” Wang wrote on X.

Wang's post followed a claim by an Anthropic researcher who said he had used the company's Claude AI model to help prove the Riemann hypothesis.
Proposed by German mathematician Bernhard Riemann in 1859, the conjecture concerns the distribution of prime numbers and is one of mathematics' best-known unsolved problems.
Wang called the development “the beginning of a significant change”.
He argued that if AI could help solve a problem of that difficulty, the main constraints would be specialised knowledge, technical infrastructure and experimentation.
